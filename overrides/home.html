Works together

One thing leads to the next.

Recipes, lists, chores and calendars usually sit in separate apps. In Prism they share one household, so something you add in one place shows up wherever it is needed.

  1. Recipe
  2. Shopping list
  3. Grocery cart

Add a recipe's ingredients to the shopping list, then send the whole list to your cart at a Kroger-family store with one tap.

  1. Chore done
  2. Points
  3. Goal reached

Kids tick off chores on the wall and a parent approves them. The points go toward a reward the kid picked.

  1. Your photos
  2. Idle screen
  3. Travel map

When nobody is using the wall, it turns into a photo frame with the time and weather, and your trips show up as pins on a globe.

Works with what you use

Your data stays where it is.

Prism shows the services your family already keeps things in, and where a service allows it, a change made in Prism goes back to it. Nobody has to move anything or keep a second copy.

Calendars

Your calendars stay in Google or iCloud. Events you add or edit on the wall change there too, and any calendar link works.

Lists

Your to-dos stay in Microsoft To Do or Google Tasks, and your shopping and wish lists in To Do. A tick on the wall ticks the item there too.

Recipes

Your recipes and meal plans stay in Tandoor or Mealie, and the shopping list goes to your Kroger cart.

Photos

Your photos stay in OneDrive or Immich and fill the wall when nobody is using it.

Install

Runs on hardware you already have.

Prism runs as a set of Docker containers. Each of these ways takes a few minutes, so pick the one that suits your setup.

Home Assistant add-on

You do not need a terminal. Add the repository in the add-on store and press Install.

Docker

Pull the pre-built image onto any Linux machine, a NAS or a Raspberry Pi 4.

Build it yourself

git clone https://github.com/sandydargoport/prism.git
cd prism && bash scripts/install.sh

Before you install

The questions people usually ask.

What does it cost?

Nothing. There is no paid tier and no subscription, and you never make an account.

Where does my family's data live?

On your own machine, in a Postgres database next to the app. You can export it from Settings > Backup.

What does it connect to?

It talks to the services you switch on (calendars, photos, weather, grocery) and makes one anonymous update check a week, which you can turn off. What it sends.

What is the licence?

Prism is free for personal and non-commercial use under PolyForm Noncommercial 1.0.0. You can run it, change it and share it; you cannot sell it or run it as a paid service.

What if the project stops?

Your install keeps working. It does not depend on any server of ours, and your data sits in a standard database that you own.

What screen do I need?

Anything with a web browser will do: a TV or monitor on the wall, a tablet, or a phone, where you can install it as an app.
