# The wall screen

Choose what the wall screen shows and where. Start from a premade layout, or arrange your own.

You can start from one of the built-in layouts, borrow one from the community gallery, or drag and resize the widgets yourself (calendar, weather, clock, meals, chores, photos and more). Each screen in the house can have its own layout.

A parent arranges layouts in the editor: the dashboard drawn on a canvas at the size of the screen that shows it, with the editing panels beside it. The editor is the same on every device, a phone included. A phone's own dashboard is a simpler page (see [On your phone](MOBILE.md)).

## Set it up

### Entering the editor

Open **More** on the dock, the row of pages at the bottom of the screen, and choose **Edit layout** under **This screen**. If the dock is tucked behind the small **Menu** tab on the bottom edge, tap the tab first. Signed out, **Edit layout** asks for a parent's PIN, and while a child is signed in it isn't there. From a page other than the dashboard it opens the screen's dashboard with the editor open.

The editor looks the same on every device. It opens on a canvas: the dashboard laid out at the size of the screen that shows it, scaled to fit the window, so what you arrange is what the wall shows. The controls sit in a column beside the canvas (below it on a phone), with one panel always open, and none of them covers the canvas: **Screen**, **Dashboard**, **Widgets**, **Through the day**, **Look** and **More**, with **Cancel** and **Done** above them.

- **On a named screen** (named in **Screen**, listed in *Settings > Screens*) **Edit layout** opens that screen's own editor, at its own size, on the dashboard it was showing. Its own settings (in **Look** and **More**) reach it when you press **Done**, and it goes back to its dashboard.
- **On a laptop or any other device** it opens at the size of a screen that shows the dashboard. **Shown at** above the canvas picks another: a named screen or a common screen size.
- **For another named screen**, **Edit** on its row in *Settings > Screens* opens the same canvas at that screen's size, and changes reach the screen on **Done**. This is how you edit from a phone, whose **More** has no **Edit layout**.

What each panel holds:

- **Dashboard**: every dashboard, to switch to one, with **New dashboard**, **Make a copy**, **Rename**, **Make the default** and **Delete** (see [Multiple dashboards](#multiple-dashboards)).
- **Widgets**: the widgets on this dashboard, the templates and the community gallery.
- **Screen**, first: the screen being edited. A **Screen** drop-down lists every named screen, set to this one; picking another opens that screen's editor, asking first when changes are not saved. The pencil beside it renames the screen in place: type the name and press Enter, or leave the field, and it is saved as *Settings > Screens* renames one; Esc keeps the old name. **Base dashboard** and **Switches** set what it shows and when it switches (see [Which dashboard a screen shows](#which-dashboard-a-screen-shows-and-when-it-switches)); **When nobody is using it** has **Rest after** and **How dark**. On a device with no name the drop-down says **This device (not named)** and **Name this device** is below it; editing a dashboard with no screen, from a phone say, it says **No screen**.
- **Through the day**: what the dashboard shows while resting and at night, and the day's line, with a dot for each of the screen's switches; tapping a switch's dot opens it in **Screen** (see [Idle, away and sitter screens](DISPLAY-MODES.md)).
- **Look**: the screen shape, **See it at a screen size**, and this screen's own settings under **This screen**.
- **More**: the overview, notices, **Export**, **Import**, **Share to the gallery** and **Reset this layout**.

A dot on a button means that panel has changes you haven't saved. **Done** saves everything and **Cancel** puts it all back.

### Glance, the built-in dashboard

Glance draws the front page as one scene over the photo, without a grid of cards. A new install starts on it.

- The date and the clock at the top left, and the weather as one line ("Partly cloudy, high 79") at the top right.
- **Next** across the middle: the event under way or about to start, in its person's colour, with its time. **Later today** follows it.
- The coming days as short sentences at the bottom left ("Tomorrow  Soccer at 10."), two things a day.
- Quiet lines at the bottom right: how many chores are left today, with a ring in each person's colour that ticks a chore off; the shopping list's count, with a plus to add an item; and, when a birthday is two to seven days away, an offer to add a task to mail a card (**Add it** or **Not now**). A line with nothing to say drops out.

In portrait the clock and weather sit on top, the day across the upper middle, the coming days below it and the quiet lines at the foot. Tap an event's words to open it; a tap anywhere else only wakes the screen. By default, while the screen rests, the sentences and quiet lines fade and **Next**, the clock and the weather stay. During the dashboard's night the ink turns warm and the photo is veiled.

Glance is edited in the same editor and stays the live scene while you edit, so each change shows at once. It places each part itself, so its **Widgets** panel has no grid. For each part (**Clock and date**, **Weather**, **The day and the coming days**, **Birthday nudge**, **Chores left** and **Shopping list**) it has **Show** and **Stays when resting**. For the whole scene:

- **Show the day as an arc**: draws the day as the sun's path in place of the list, with each timed event as a bead in its person's colour. Off by default.
- **Days ahead**: how many days the sentences cover, from 1 to 7.
- **Text size**: Normal, Large or Larger.

In the **Dashboard** panel Glance comes first, marked **Built in**. **Reset to default** puts its parts back as Glance first had them. It can't be renamed or deleted. **Make a copy** saves its widgets as a dashboard of your own, which you arrange on the grid like any other. An install without Glance lists it there with **Add**.

### Built-in templates

In the **Widgets** panel, **Start from a template** loads a ready-made arrangement for you to rearrange. It asks first, since it replaces every widget on the dashboard, with its place, fill, outline and text. There are six templates, each in a landscape and a portrait version, and the panel lists the ones for the current screen shape. The screensaver has its own set (see below).

Every template follows the same plan. One widget is the hero of the board, usually the calendar; on photo boards the wallpaper itself is the hero. The other widgets are sized to their natural shape and grouped into two or three balanced zones, instead of an even grid of four.

| Template | What it's for | Widgets |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Family Central** | The everyday board. | Calendar hero, a tall rail of upcoming events and birthdays, and a weather and clock strip. |
| **Calendar Focus** | When the schedule matters most. | A full-height calendar hero with a slim weather and clock rail. |
| **Command Center** | Everything on one screen. | Calendar hero plus weather, clock, messages, and columns for tasks and chores. |
| **Meal Planner** | A board for the kitchen. | Meals hero, a tall shopping list column, and a weather and clock corner. |
| **School Mornings** | A board for getting out the door. | Calendar hero, a bus departure countdown, weather, homework tasks and the lunch menu. |
| **Ambient** | Photos first. | Mostly empty on purpose: a couple of glass clock and weather accents float over open space so a photo **wallpaper** shows through as the hero. Use it with a photo wallpaper. |

Most templates fill the canvas exactly. **Ambient** leaves the middle open on purpose for the wallpaper.

> A brand-new empty dashboard gets an internal **Default** layout built around the weather. A fresh install has no calendar connected yet, so that layout leaves out the calendar hero. It isn't one of the templates.

### Community gallery

In the **Widgets** panel, **Browse the community gallery** shows dashboard layouts that other Prism users have shared. The gallery loads a community index and shows each layout as a card with a live thumbnail, its name, description, author, widget count and orientation.

- **Search**: type in the search box and press **Enter** to match layouts by name or description. The **×** button clears the box.
- **Filter by orientation**: tap **▭ Landscape** or **▯ Portrait** to see only those layouts, and tap again to clear it. The filter starts on the orientation of the dashboard you are editing. Layouts stretch to fill whatever screen shows them, so the exact resolution makes no difference. Orientation does matter, because a portrait layout gets bars down the sides on a landscape screen, and the other way round.
- **Preview**: each card draws a small copy of the board on a neutral "photo field", turned to fit so portrait boards don't spill over, with a badge for the orientation.
- **Apply layout**: puts that design on your dashboard, after asking, since it replaces every widget and its fill, outline and text.
- **Clear filters**: appears when a search or orientation filter finds nothing.

While you arrange the screensaver, the gallery shows screensaver layouts instead.

#### Sharing your layout with the community

To share a board you like, open the **More** panel and choose **Share to the gallery**. Fill in a name, author, description and orientation, and choose **Open GitHub Issue**. Prism opens a filled-in submission for you, from any instance including hosted ones. Once it is approved, your layout shows up in everyone's community gallery.

You need a GitHub account to submit to the gallery. Without one, use **Download file** in the same dialog and send the file any way you like, and the other household loads it with **Import**.

#### Copy between the dashboard and the screensaver

**More > Use while resting** replaces the resting screen's layout with the dashboard you are editing. **Done** keeps it and **Cancel** puts the old one back. While the editor shows the resting screen the same item reads **Use as the dashboard**. Both need photos or the night sky behind the idle screen, because a dashboard that acts as its own idle screen has no separate layout. The screensaver can show fewer kinds of widget than the dashboard, so any it can't show are left out, and the confirmation lists them.

#### Export & Import

- **Export** (More > Export) copies your current layout as JSON to give to someone directly. Where the page can't copy, it saves a `.json` file instead.
- **Share to the gallery > Download file** saves the layout as a `.json` file.
- **Import** (More > Import) takes pasted layout JSON, or use **Choose file…** to load a downloaded one.

### Multiple dashboards

You can make a separate dashboard for each room or display. The **Dashboard** panel lists every dashboard; tap one to edit it.

- **New dashboard** asks for a name and what to start from: the Default Template, a copy of the current dashboard, or blank.
- **Make a copy** saves the dashboard you are editing as a new one, or over another one.
- **Rename**, **Make the default** and **Delete** act on the dashboard you are editing.
- The **default** dashboard lives at **`/`**. **Make the default** makes any dashboard the default.
- Named dashboards get URLs like **`/d/kitchen`** or **`/d/living-room`**.
- Each dashboard has its own widget layout, screensaver layout and orientation.
- Bookmark a dashboard URL on a device that only shows that dashboard, so it opens straight to it.

#### Which dashboard a screen shows, and when it switches

A named screen shows one dashboard, and can switch to another for a while. Both are set in the screen's own editor, in **Screen** (More > Edit layout > Screen): **Base dashboard** is its dashboard, shown except while a switch is on, and each switch is a row of its own under **Switches**. Two screens can show the same dashboard, and each keeps its own switches.

- **At bus time** follows your bus routes: from 30 minutes before each route's time at the stop to 15 minutes after, on the route's days. A route due at 7:45 on weekdays switches the screen from 7:15 to 8:00. Change the route's time and the switch moves with it. The row lists the windows it will use. It still switches on a school holiday, because routes have no holiday calendar.
  - **Routes**: with two or more bus routes, the row has a chip for each, all on to begin with. Turn one off to leave that route out. With every route on, the switch also follows routes added later. One always stays on.
  - **From** so many minutes **before to** so many **after**: how long before the route's time the switch starts and how long after it ends, from 0 to 120 minutes.
  - A screen can have more than one bus switch, for example the morning route to one dashboard and the afternoon route to another.
- **At set times** has a from and a to, and its days (Monday to Sunday, any set). Times use the household time zone. One that ends before it starts runs past midnight, and one that starts and ends at the same time lasts all day.

**+ Switch at set times** and, if bus tracking is set up (at least one route in *Settings > Bus*), **+ Switch at bus time** add one; a bus switch made before keeps its row either way; a screen can have up to 12. When two overlap the higher one wins, and the arrow on a row moves it up. **Open** edits that dashboard. Nothing reaches the screen until **Done**, and **Cancel** puts it all back. In the **Dashboard** panel, the dashboard the screen shows is marked **Shown here**, so editing another one never changes what the screen shows. *Settings > Screens* also picks a screen's dashboard from its row.

A device nobody named shows the default dashboard and never switches. To give it switches, name it: open the editor on it, then **Screen** > **Name this device**.

Make a dashboard to switch to with **Make a copy** in the Dashboard panel and rearrange it there. When a switch starts or ends, the screen changes over in place: widgets that are on both dashboards slide to their new spots and the others fade. The change waits until nobody has touched the screen for a minute, and it never happens while someone is editing the layout. If you open another dashboard by hand, the screen keeps it until the next switch starts or ends. With reduced motion turned on the dashboards crossfade, and at the **Minimal** performance level they swap instantly.

#### Notices

A named screen can show small notice cards down the top right of its dashboard. In the editor's **More** panel, **Notices** turns each kind on and sets how many minutes ahead it looks:

- **Bus**: the bus is due at your stop within the set minutes, using the live position where there is one and the emailed estimate otherwise. The card goes once the bus has passed the stop.
- **Event**: a timed event starts within the set minutes. The card goes when the event starts. All-day events never show.
- **Message**: someone posted a family message within the set minutes.

The newest card is on top, and up to four show at once. Prism checks for new messages every half minute and for events every minute. Tap the **×** on a card to dismiss it. The dashboard underneath stays where it is, and the cards cover it until they go. Cards only appear on dashboards. They also show over the screensaver, where they turn dark, keep updating and dim along with it.

#### Saving

- **Done** saves the dashboard you are editing, with its screensaver layout.
- **Make a copy** in the Dashboard panel saves it as a new dashboard.
- **More > Reset this layout** puts the default arrangement in place of yours: the **Default** layout on a dashboard, or the default screensaver while you arrange the screensaver. Nothing is saved until **Done**, and **Cancel** brings yours back.

### Orientation

**Screen shape** in the **Look** panel switches between **Landscape** and **Portrait**. It flips the design canvas between the 48×27 landscape frame and the 36×64 portrait frame, so you can lay out each orientation separately to match how the display is mounted.

## Use it

### The layout grid

Prism places widgets on a fixed **design canvas** instead of your screen's actual pixels, so one design looks right on many screens:

- **Landscape** canvas: **48 columns × 27 rows** (16:9).
- **Portrait** canvas: **36 columns × 64 rows** (9:16).

On the live dashboard the box around your widgets is stretched to fill the real screen. A layout that covers the whole canvas therefore fills the display edge to edge at any resolution.

#### Placing and sizing widgets

- **Drag** a widget to move it on the grid.
- **Tap** a widget to select it; it is marked **Move**. Tap it again for resize mode, marked **Resize**, and drag the handles on its edges and corners. A third tap lets it go.
- With a widget selected, **Delete** or **Backspace** removes it, with **Undo** in the message that follows. The arrow keys move it one cell, or in resize mode make it wider or taller (right, down) or narrower or shorter (left, up). No key acts while you type in a field or hold Ctrl, Alt or Cmd.
- In the **Widgets** panel, **On this dashboard** lists each widget with its **X**, **Y**, **W** and **H**, for exact placement. Tap a widget's name there to pick it, as a tap on the screen does; the **×** beside the name hides it, and **+ Add widget** brings back a hidden one. A widget added new takes the fill, outline and text the other widgets all share; where they differ, it starts with the theme's default.

The widgets are **Clock, Weather, Calendar, Tasks, Chores, Shopping, Meals, Messages, Photos, Points/Goals, Birthdays, Wishes, and Bus Tracker.**

#### Widget properties

Pick a widget on the screen or in the list in the **Widgets** panel (**More > Edit layout > Widgets**); its settings are below the list, beside the canvas on a laptop and below it on a phone. Its row in the list is marked. If another panel is open, Widgets comes forward. With nothing picked, that place says so in one line. The settings style that widget on its own, and the canvas shows only the screen. The **×** beside the widget's name lets it go, as a third tap on it does. With the focus on a setting, **Delete**, **Backspace** and the arrow keys still act on the widget.

- **Colours**: which set of colours the Fill, Outline and Text swatches offer (Seasonal, Ocean, Sunset, Forest, Mono or Candy).
- **Fill**: **Default** (the theme's card), **Transparent** (no card: the words sit straight on the photo in light text with a soft shadow), **Frosted** (a white, blurred card), **Glass** (a see-through card over the photo, which the Ambient templates use), a colour from the palette, or **Custom**. Under the swatches, **Opacity** sets how strong the fill is, or **Blur** for Frosted.
- **Outline**: the border's colour and opacity, including **None** to remove the widget's border.
- **Text**: the text colour, and **Size** (S, M, L or XL).
- **Apply to all widgets**, under each of **Fill**, **Outline** and **Text**: puts this widget's fill, outline, or text colour and size on every widget of the dashboard, hidden ones too. The line under it says how many widgets differ, or that every widget already matches. To turn outlines off everywhere, pick **None** under **Outline** on one widget, then **Apply to all widgets**.
- **Align**, for the clock and the weather, under **Text**: **Across** (Auto, Left, Centre, Right) and **Up/down** (Auto, Top, Middle, Bottom) set where the contents sit. On **Auto**, a small tile (a third of the width or less) that touches a screen corner hugs that corner, and any other tile is centred. A small weather tile shows the icon, temperature and condition on one line each, and the rest once the tile is wide enough.
- **Grid**, for the calendar: the strength of its grid lines and a background colour for its cells.
- **Hide when empty**: Prism doesn't draw the widget while it has nothing to show (no open tasks, no messages, an empty shopping list, no birthdays coming up, no meals planned) and brings it back when something arrives. Its space stays blank, so nothing else moves. It is on by default on the screensaver and on a calm dashboard's idle screen, and off by default on a dashboard in use. Clock, Weather, Calendar, Photos and Travel always have something to show, so they don't have this option. In the editor the widget always shows, marked **Empty: hidden** when the live screen leaves it out.
- **Hide first on a small screen**: leaves the widget out on a screen below about three quarters of the design size, so the others have room.

### Overview and checks

The **More** panel's **Overview** has a small map of the whole layout. It shows where each widget sits and flags problems such as widgets that overlap or are too small. Click anywhere on the map to scroll the canvas there.

Under it, a preview shows how your one design looks on each common screen size. That way you can check that one layout works on all your displays without keeping a separate design for each resolution.

### Preview mode

Choose **See it at a screen size** in the **Look** panel, or press **Ctrl+Shift+M**, to hide the editor's controls for a moment and see the board as it will really look. Click **Exit Preview** to go back. **Show Nav** and **Hide Nav** let you check it with and without the dock, which helps when tuning a layout for a wall display.

Each screen chooses how its dock behaves on a dashboard with **Auto-hide navigation**, in the editor's **Look** panel under **This screen**. On, the dock tucks behind a small **Menu** tab on the bottom edge and comes back with a tap. Off, it stays on screen and the dashboard ends above it.

### Screensaver layout

Each dashboard has its own screensaver layout, used when photos or the night sky show while the screen rests. In the editor, open **Through the day**, tap **See it** on the **Resting** row (or step to night on the day's line), then open **Widgets** to arrange it. The templates and the community gallery switch to their screensaver sets as well.

The screen rests after the time set as **Rest after** in the Screen panel: 30 seconds, 1 minute, 2 minutes, 10 minutes, 1 hour or **Never rests**, 2 minutes by default. The screensaver templates keep the calendar, or tonight's meals, as the hero, with small clock, weather and message accents over one clear area of the photo so the wallpaper stays the main thing.

See [Idle, away and sitter screens](DISPLAY-MODES.md) for more on the screensaver and Away mode.

## Details

### Troubleshooting

#### Edit layout is missing from More

Only parents can edit layouts. While a child is signed in, **More** has no **Edit layout**: sign out, or sign in as a parent. A phone's **More** never has it; edit the screen from *Settings > Screens* instead.

#### My layout doesn't fill the screen / leaves a gap

The live board stretches the box around your widgets to fill the display. If a layout leaves a gap, the widgets usually don't reach the edge of the canvas. Stretch the outermost widgets to the last row or column, or start from a template that fills the whole canvas.

#### Clicks at the top of the screen don't respond under browser fullscreen (F11)

In F11 fullscreen the browser keeps the top edge of the screen for its own controls, and that can swallow clicks on whatever sits there. Install Prism as an app, or just maximize the window, instead of using F11.

#### A community layout applied but looks empty

A community layout only holds widget positions and none of your data. Widgets such as the calendar or meals stay empty until that feature has data, for example once a calendar is connected.
